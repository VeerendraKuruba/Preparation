# Behavioral / Googleyness & Leadership — Google

> Google's behavioral interview is called "Googleyness & Leadership" (G&L).
> Use the **STAR method**: Situation → Task → Action → Result.
> Senior (L5) bar: show impact beyond yourself — cross-team, mentoring, driving org-level changes.

---

## Core Dimensions Assessed

| Dimension | What they look for |
|-----------|--------------------|
| **Technical Leadership** | Led design decisions, influenced architecture, drove consensus |
| **End-to-End Ownership** | Designed, built, launched, and maintained — not just coded |
| **Ambiguity Handling** | Defined scope when unclear, asked right clarifying questions |
| **Collaboration** | Worked through conflict, aligned stakeholders, lifted teammates |
| **Learning from Failure** | Acknowledged mistakes, changed behavior, demonstrated growth |
| **Googleyness** | Comfortable with ambiguity, collaborative, not defensive |

---

## Most Commonly Asked Behavioral Questions at Google

### Leadership & Influence
1. Tell me about a time you **led a project** without formal authority.
2. Describe a time you had to **make a difficult technical decision** with incomplete information.
3. Tell me about a time you **influenced a team or organization** to adopt a new approach.
4. Tell me about a time you **proactively proposed a change** that improved the team/product.
5. Describe a project where you took **end-to-end ownership** — from idea to launch.

### Conflict & Collaboration
6. Tell me about a **conflict with a coworker** — how did you handle it?
7. Describe a time you **disagreed with your manager** or tech lead. What did you do?
8. Tell me about a time you had to **build consensus** among people with competing priorities.
9. Describe a situation where you had to work with a **difficult stakeholder**.

### Ambiguity & Problem Solving
10. Tell me about a time you worked on a **poorly defined problem**. How did you define it?
11. Describe a time you had to **make a decision with limited data**. What was your approach?
12. Tell me about a time the **requirements changed mid-project**. How did you handle it?

### Failure & Learning
13. Tell me about a time you **failed**. What did you learn?
14. Describe a time you **missed a deadline**. What happened and what changed?
15. Tell me about a **bug or incident you caused** in production. How did you handle it?

### Mentorship & Growth
16. Tell me about a time you **mentored a junior engineer**.
17. Describe a situation where you **helped improve your team's processes or practices**.
18. Tell me about a time you gave someone **difficult feedback**.

### Prioritization & Execution
19. Tell me about a time you had to **prioritize between competing projects**.
20. Describe a project where you had to **cut scope** to meet a deadline without hurting quality.

## STAR Answer Template

```
Situation: Set the context briefly (1-2 sentences)
  - "We were building a new checkout flow for 10M users, 3 weeks before launch..."

Task: What was your specific responsibility?
  - "I was the only frontend engineer and had to own the entire client-side architecture..."

Action: What did YOU specifically do? (Most important — use "I", not "we")
  - "I proposed a risk analysis of the two approaches, organized a design review,
     and ultimately convinced the team to adopt SSR for the checkout flow because..."

Result: Quantify the outcome
  - "We shipped on time, page load dropped from 4s to 1.2s, and conversion rate
     improved by 12% in the first month."
```

## Example Prepared Stories (Fill In With Your Own)

### Story 1: Technical Leadership Without Authority
- **Situation**: [Your project — describe scale and stakes]
- **Task**: You identified a critical architectural problem but had no formal authority to change it
- **Action**: You analyzed the alternatives, prepared a comparison doc, got buy-in from key engineers, and presented to management
- **Result**: Proposal was adopted, measurable improvement (performance, reliability, velocity)

### Story 2: Handling Conflict
- **Situation**: Disagreement with another engineer on approach (e.g., state management strategy, API design)
- **Task**: Needed to align the team and move forward
- **Action**: Scheduled 1:1, listened to their perspective, found common ground, suggested a time-boxed experiment to validate the approach
- **Result**: Team reached consensus, no relationship damage, delivered on time

### Story 3: Ambiguity & Ownership
- **Situation**: Vague requirement ("make the app faster") with no defined goals
- **Task**: Define measurable success criteria and an action plan
- **Action**: Ran performance audit (Lighthouse, DevTools), identified top 3 bottlenecks, proposed sprint plan with milestones
- **Result**: Shipped improvements that reduced LCP by X%, reduced bundle size by Y%

### Story 4: Failure & Recovery
- **Situation**: Pushed a change that caused a production incident
- **Task**: Own the issue and recover quickly
- **Action**: Immediately rolled back, wrote a post-mortem, identified root cause (missing test case / race condition), added regression tests and better monitoring
- **Result**: Incident resolved in 45 min, no repeat, new runbook in place

## Google-Specific Behavioral Tips

- **"Googleyness"** is about being **collaborative, humble, and data-driven**, not about Google tools
- Show that you **seek feedback** and don't take criticism personally
- Demonstrate **comfort with ambiguity** — show you lean in, not wait for clarity
- For L5: stories must show **impact on team or org**, not just individual contributions
- Have **3-4 strong stories** that can be adapted to answer different questions
- Mention **metrics** wherever possible: load time, conversion rate, dev velocity, error rate

## Questions to Ask the Interviewer

Good questions show you think about the role strategically:

1. "What does the first 90 days look like for a senior frontend engineer on your team?"
2. "How does the team balance feature velocity with technical debt?"
3. "What frontend performance challenges is the team currently facing?"
4. "How are technical decisions made — is it top-down or consensus-driven?"
5. "What does engineering leadership at L5 look like on your team day-to-day?"
