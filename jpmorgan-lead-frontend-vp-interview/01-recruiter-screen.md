# Round 1: Recruiter Phone Screen

**Duration:** 30 minutes  
**Format:** Phone or video call  
**Goal:** Background validation, culture fit check, logistics alignment  
**Elimination:** No — but poor impression can slow the process

---

## What to Expect

- Walk through your resume/experience
- "Why JP Morgan?" and "Why this role?"
- Your current compensation and expectations
- Availability and timeline
- No technical questions at this stage

---

## Key Questions & Answers

### Q: Walk me through your background.

**Answer Framework:**
Start with your current role, highlight scale, then connect to why JPMC is the natural next step.

> "I'm currently a Lead/Senior Frontend Engineer with X years of experience, primarily building large-scale React applications. I've led teams of [N] engineers, architected design systems, and worked closely with product and design stakeholders. Most recently I [specific achievement — e.g., led a migration to micro-frontends serving 500K daily users, or built a component library used by 20+ teams]. I'm excited about JP Morgan because of the scale of their engineering challenges — specifically how they're building products like the Manhattan Design System for thousands of engineers, and contributing open-source tools like Salt DS and Perspective. I want to work on frontend problems that have real financial impact at a global scale."

---

### Q: Why JP Morgan Chase specifically?

**Answer (tailor to your background):**

> "A few reasons stand out to me. First, JP Morgan is one of the most ambitious engineering organizations in finance — they're not just a bank, they're a global technology company that happens to be a bank. Second, I'm genuinely interested in the frontend challenges at their scale — their Manhattan Design System serves roughly 3,000 engineers, which is the kind of design system problem I find deeply interesting. Third, their open-source contributions like Salt DS and Perspective show a commitment to quality and the broader engineering community, which I value in an employer. And lastly, at VP level, the opportunity to lead and shape frontend architecture across multiple products is something I'm actively seeking."

---

### Q: Why are you looking to leave your current role?

**Safe, positive answers:**
- Seeking greater scale and impact
- Want to work on more complex technical problems (financial systems)
- Excited by the leadership scope at VP level
- Want to influence frontend architecture at an organizational level

**Avoid:** Criticizing current employer, mentioning internal politics, or sounding desperate.

---

### Q: What is your current compensation and expectations?

**Tip:** Research VP-level JPMC compensation via levels.fyi and Glassdoor first.

- JPMC VP Frontend typically: **$180K–$250K base + bonus (15–20%) + RSUs**
- State a range slightly above your target
- Say you're looking for a total compensation package in line with the market rate for VP-level roles in [your city]

---

### Q: Are you interviewing elsewhere?

**Answer:**
> "Yes, I'm in conversations with a couple of other firms, but JP Morgan is definitely my top priority given the scale of the role and the types of engineering challenges involved."

This creates gentle urgency without bluffing.

---

### Q: What is your timeline?

Be honest — if you need 3 weeks, say so. Recruiters appreciate predictability.

---

## Preparation Checklist

- [ ] 2-minute "walk me through your background" pitch memorized
- [ ] "Why JP Morgan?" answer ready (connects to MDS, Salt DS, financial scale)
- [ ] Compensation range researched on levels.fyi
- [ ] Know your notice period and earliest start date
- [ ] Have 2–3 questions ready to ask the recruiter about the team, process, timeline
